1 Introduction
1.1 Background
This document specifies the reporting instructions for the provision of data necessary to support
the integrity of crypto-asset markets, including market abuse supervision under Title VI of MiCA.
The primary purpose of these instructions is to define the file formats, submission process,
naming convention, validation rules, and data content required from Crypto-Asset Service
Providers (CASPs) to provide to the MiCA Digital Assets Surveillance system (MIDAS).

MIDAS is the system designed to ingest data from CASPs to be used for the monitoring of market
abuse and to support overall market integrity under MiCA. It is based on the following legal
provisions:

   1. The Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 on Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA), came into force in
      June 2023, with the provisions applicable to CASPs applying from 30 December 2024.
      Within its provisions, Article 94 of MiCA, provides that in order to perform its duties under
      Titles II to VI of the same Regulation, National Competent Authorities (NCAs) are granted
      relevant powers, including the power to require any person to provide information and
      documents that they consider could be relevant for the performance of their duties. This
      provision is to be read in conjunction with the provisions regarding the record keeping
      obligation under MiCA Articles 68(9) and 76(15), which are functional to NCAs’ fulfilment
      of their supervisory and market integrity tasks.

   2. Article 68(9) of MiCA holds that CASPs shall keep records of all crypto-asset services,
      activities, orders, and transactions. The detailed record-keeping requirements are
      specified in the RTS 2025/1140, which defines the records to be maintained, including a
      sub-set of on-chain data elements. These records shall be sufficient to enable competent
      authorities to fulfil their supervisory tasks and to take enforcement measures, and in
      particular to ascertain whether CASPs have complied with all obligations including those
      with respect to clients or prospective clients and to the integrity of the market.

   3. Moreover, in accordance with Article 76(15) of MiCA, CASPs operating a trading platform
      for crypto-assets shall keep at the disposal of NCAs, for at least five years, the relevant
      data relating to all orders in crypto-assets that are advertised through the systems of
      CASPs operating a trading platform, or give access to the NCA, to monitor the trading
      activity. This data also includes a sub-set of on-chain data elements.

ESMA has no formal supervisory role in MiCA. MiCA assigns supervisory and enforcement
responsibilities to NCAs. Therefore, NCAs remain responsible for day-to-day market surveillance,
investigations and enforcement. They are the authorities that decide whether to follow up on an

                                                                                                 8
alert or a data quality issue, request additional information, launch an investigation, or impose
sanctions. However, with MIDAS, the NCAs collectively decided to centralise the technical
capability needed to collect and analyse data on crypto-assets markets, which are inherently
cross-border and technologically complex and require a common analytical foundation to monitor
the markets at EU-level as no single authority can effectively do that in isolation at local level.
This decision has been implemented via a “Delegation Agreement”, which is a tool envisaged
under Article 28 of ESMA regulation1 that allows NCAs to delegate some of their tasks to ESMA.
A delegation can only cover tasks and not supervisory responsibilities. Under the delegated
project, NCAs have delegated to ESMA the tasks of collecting data, processing it, performing
quality checks, calculating common indicators of suspicious Market Abuse behaviours (alerts)
and distributing those alerts to the relevant NCAs. Following a public procurement process, ESMA
has contracted a third-party specialised provider for this delegated project.

For MIDAS, the data collection framework does not mandate the transmission of the full set of
records subject to the record-keeping obligations under Articles 68(9) and 76(15) of MiCA.
Instead, under the Delegation Agreements executed between NCAs and ESMA, MIDAS operates
exclusively on a defined subset of data elements necessary for market monitoring and the
detection of market abuse. This subset was subsequently extended by the Delegated Project
Board to include additional pre-trade data fields, specifically order side, buy or sell indicators, and
order quantities including cancellations. This targeted expansion aims to improve alert calibration
and minimize false positives, while ensuring that data collection remains limited to what is strictly
necessary for supervisory purposes. The precise scope of these requirements is implemented in
phases, starting with a smaller subset of data and gradually increasing the scope as supervisors
build knowledge and experience in crypto markets, and as CASPs build their implementation
capacity.

1.2 Scope
The scope of this document covers the reporting instructions for the submission of a defined
subset of data to the MIDAS tool. In alignment with the Delegation Agreements executed with
ESMA, this data collection is divided into two distinct functional categories based on the reporting
entity's regulatory obligations under MiCA:

   1. Executions Data (Record-Keeping Data auth.117): This category covers executed
      transactions. Submission of this data subset is mandatory for all CASPs operating within
      the EU regulatory framework under the record-keeping obligations of MiCA Article 68(9)
      and are specified in the RTS 2025/1140.

   2. Orderbook Data (auth.118): This category covers the pre-trade order lifecycle data.
      Submission of this data subset is mandatory only for CASPs operating a trading

2 System overview
The MIDAS system acts as a central hub for crypto-asset market surveillance. CASPs are
responsible for implementing submission processes to provide data in the specified JSON format.

   1. Submission: CASPs upload JSON files to a dedicated SFTP drop point.

   2. Validation: Files undergo three levels of check: Transmission, File and Content
      validations.

   3. Alerting: The system applies detection algorithms to identify potential market abuse and
      generate alerts for NCAs.

3 Main principles
3.1 Reporting principles
MIDAS follows a phased implementation approach aimed at limiting implementation costs,
complexity and operational risk. Under its first phase, MIDAS will collect only a limited set of on-
and off-chain data elements necessary for EU-level market abuse monitoring and alert
generation3. Consequently, MIDAS does not collect the full set of records subject to the record-
keeping obligations under Articles 68(9) and 76(15) of MiCA. In line with its phased
implementation approach, the first phase excludes personal data, non-public CASP transaction
and supervisory data, as well as information collected for enforcement or investigative purposes
(such as STORs and RFIs). Future implementation phases will extend the scope of MIDAS to
include supervisory data, including personal identifiers, where required for supervisory purposes.

CASPs operating a trading platform must submit both record keeping executions data
(auth.117) and pre trade orderbook data (auth.118). In record keeping executions both buy and
sell legs must be included in the same record (if applicable).

All other CASPS must only submit record keeping executions data (auth.117). Only relevant buy
or sell leg must be included in one record.

3.3 File naming convention
To facilitate automated ingestion, MIDAS requires a strict naming convention and complete
submission of the data. Files that deviate from this format will be automatically rejected.

Naming convention: <FileType>_<DateDDMMYYYY>_<partN>_ <partM>.json

   •   FileType: Specifies the message type. Possible FileType values are
       CryptoAssetRecordKeeping and CryptoAssetOrderBook.

   •   DateDDMMYYYY: The record keeping date the data refers to (e.g., 15052026).

   •   partN: For data larger than 500 MB, the file must be split into multiple parts to stay under
       the size limit. To preserve the order, a sequential suffix for multi-part submissions is
       introduced and it must increase sequentially (e.g., _1, _2). No periods or special
       characters other than underscore should precede the suffix of the filename(s). The
       minimum value of the suffix must be 1 and there is no upper limit in the maximum number.
       The partN numbers must be reset for each <DateDDMMYYYY>.

   •   partM: Suffix denoting the total number of split files for a Date and Filetype. For data
       smaller than 500 MB, the suffix will be 1 as the dataset only consists of one file. No periods
       or special characters other than underscore should precede the suffix of the filename(s).
       The minimum value of the suffix must be 1 and there is no upper limit. The partM number
       must be reset for each combination of <FileType> and <DateDDMMYYYY> as it always
       represents the total number of files for this specific submission.

extension, or unrecognized FileType) will be rejected as part of the validation process.

3.4 Data collection
CASPs are required to submit their data files every two weeks to a designated SFTP location
mapped directly to the MIDAS system. Should a CASP prefer to submit data at a different
frequency, this can be accommodated upon agreement with the MIDAS provider and the NCAs.
All submissions must be provided exclusively in plain text JSON format. All files submitted in any
other formats will be automatically rejected by the system.

3.5 Data validation
MIDAS applies a structured, multi-layered validation process to ensure that all incoming data is
complete, accurate, consistent, and aligned with predefined schemas before it is made available
for downstream processing, analytics, and reporting. This process is designed to proactively
identify data quality issues early, reduce operational risk, and provide clear visibility into any errors
or anomalies detected during ingestion.

3.5.1 Data transmission/file validation rules
Once a file is ingested in the system, MIDAS performs a series of structural checks to confirm
that the file can be reliably interpreted and processed. These initial checks determine whether an
issue triggers a total file-level rejection.

    3.5.1.1JSON file Validation
    •   File Naming Convention: MIDAS reviews the JSON file name to ensure alignment with
        the                  following                   naming                convention:
        <FileType>_<DateDDMMYYYY>_<partN>_<partM>.json. Submitted files failing to match
        this naming convention are immediately rejected.

    •   JSON Schema: The JSON file is validated against the published JSON schema provided
        in




                                                                                                      49
   •   Annex 2: File format: JSON schema. If a file is structurally malformed, corrupted, breaks
       core JSON syntax rules, or violates the JSON schema, then the entire file will be rejected,
       and no records within will be processed.

Only the fields used for the generation of alert mechanisms will be subsequently ingested and
validated by MIDAS.

    3.5.1.2Required Fields Presence
MIDAS verifies that all fields required for the generation of MIDAS alert mechanisms, as defined
in the JSON schema, are structurally present for each record block within the submitted JSON
file.

3.5.2 Data content validation rules
For each individual record in the submitted JSON file that passed the validation checks described
in section 3.5.1 Data transmission/file validation rules, MIDAS validates the content of every field
to ensure data accuracy at the field level. The system validates each field against data types,
expected logical ranges, internal dependencies, and external standard registries. If a content
validation rule is triggered for a record, then that specific record will be rejected.

    3.5.2.1Field Format Validation
   •   Mandatory fields: The system verifies that all mandatory field values required for alert
       production are present in each record. Mandatory execution and orderbook fields marked
       as required cannot be null. If a missing value is submitted for a required field, a validation
       error is triggered, and the individual record will be rejected.

   •   Data type: Each field value is checked to ensure it matches its defined data type. For
       numerical price and quantity fields, pattern checks are executed to ensure alignment with
       maximum decimal digit criteria, preventing records with malformed numerical inputs from
       entering the system.

   •   String length constraints: Text type fields must strictly obey defined length thresholds.
       Any record containing values exceeding these structural boundaries will be automatically
       rejected or flagged as a data quality warning.

   •   Pre-defined set of values are verified directly at the JSON schema level. This structural
       schema check ensures that these fields (e.g. Buy-sell indicator, Validity period, Order type
       classification, Passive or aggressive indicator) contain only valid pre-defined values. Field
       values deviating from these enumerations will break structural schema conformance and
       trigger a record rejection.

                                                                                                  50
3.5.2.2Date and Time Validation
•   Date and timestamp fields are validated to ensure temporal accuracy and consistency:
       ○ Correctness of the format.
       ○ Values within acceptable date ranges as defined by the system.

•   All date and time values are expected to be submitted with millisecond precision. If the
    ingestion adapter for a CASP is configured to parse a specific sub-second mask (e.g.
    YYYY-MM-DD HH:mm:ss.fff), all the submitted records that drop this precise formatting
    result in an automatic record rejection.

•   In addition, the following temporal dependency rules apply:
        ○ When Validity period is set to GTDV, the Validity period and time field is mandatory
            and cannot be null or empty. Records where this condition is not met will be
            rejected.
        ○ Files where a significant proportion of Trading date time values are set to midnight
            (00:00:00.000) are flagged as a data quality warning, as this pattern is indicative
            of a timestamp defaulting issue in the upstream system.

3.5.2.3Value Constraints
    Numeric fields (e.g. Quantity, Price, and Initial quantity) that are expected to be non-
    negative are validated to ensure that values are positive, helping to catch logical or
    calculation-related data issues. In case a negative value is detected in fields specifying
    absolute execution quantities, order volumes, or market prices, the individual record is
    rejected. For Remaining quantity and Traded quantity fields, a value of zero is permitted
    but they must not contain negative values.

•   Sequential and Macro-Ratio Protections: The system performs statistical and time-series
    monitoring during data load.
       ○ The system compares the mix of Status values in each submitted file against the
           historical patterns observed for the same crypto-asset service provider. Files
           whose composition deviates significantly from the entity's established reporting
           behaviour are flagged for review. This includes cases where a status that is
           normally present is missing from the submission, which may indicate that an event
           type is not being captured upstream.
       ○ Price Jump Protection: The system runs chronological sequence checks on
           execution prices for the same asset; records demonstrating a price variance jump
           of 1,000% or greater between consecutive entries under the same Symbol are
           automatically flagged for review.



                                                                                            51
    •   Fields tied to established international operational consensus standards are validated
        against active official global registers:
            ○ Market Identification Codes (MIC): Segment MIC is verified against the official ISO
                10383 directory, requiring a valid segment MIC (or an operating MIC if a distinct
                segment code is unavailable). Explicit regulatory exceptions are allowed for XOFF
                and XXXX. For orderbook records this check applies only when Transaction
                identification code field is populated. For execution records it applies
                unconditionally.
            ○ Currency Classifications: Fiat currency representations must resolve exactly
                against the active ISO 4217 standard currency codes.
            ○ Digital Token Identifiers (DTI): Digital asset tracking fields must map directly to the
                official ISO 24165-2. Records failing this check are flagged as a data quality
                warning.

    3.5.2.4Field Dependency Validation
The system enforces cross-field relational dependencies based on transaction statuses. This
includes verifying that:

    •   Transaction identification code is populated when Status is FILL or PARF and remains
        completely empty for all other statuses.
    •   Traded quantity, Transaction price, Trading platform for crypto-asset are populated if and
        only if Transaction identification code is populated.
    •   Traded quantity and Remaining quantity do not mathematically exceed the Initial quantity.
    •   When Status is FILL or PARF, fields like Remaining quantity are not null. Furthermore, if
        the order is a true limit order (Order type classification = LMTO), Limit price and
        Transaction price must be populated, with Transaction price falling strictly within the
        boundaries established by the Limit price.
    •   The Passive or Aggressive Indicator field must remain completely empty unless the Status
        is FILL or PARF.
    •   For trading venues, both matched trades must be submitted as one record, including both
        the Buy leg (with the Buyer alternative identification element populated) and the Sell leg
        (with the Seller alternative identification element populated).

The above will result in a record rejection or warning depending on the case (see Annex 3: Data
validations for the full details).

    3.5.2.5Duplicate Detection
MIDAS identifies duplicate records to prevent double-counting or inconsistent reporting. If a file
contains duplicate records, only the unique records will be processed. The duplicates will be
ignored, but the file itself will still be processed for valid records. The trailing duplicate records will
be rejected.



                                                                                                        52
3.5.3 Feedback files
After processing each uploaded file, a feedback file is generated and written to the response
directory of the CASP’s SFTP account, typically within 2 hours of receipt. CASPs retrieve it using
the same SFTP connection used to submit data. This file contains:

   •   Transmission/file validation feedback: In case a file fails the transmission/file validation
       rules defined in section 7.1 Transmission/file Validation Rules then the file will be rejected
       and no records will be processed. CASPs must correct the file and resubmit it in full.

   •   Record-level (content) validation feedback: In case specific records fail the content
       checks defined in section 7.2 Content Validation Rules, the feedback identifies the record
       and the content validation rule violated. For submissions containing multiple records,
       content validation is performed on a record level. Therefore, some records may be
       accepted while others may be rejected. The feedback provided is specific to each
       individual record and identifies the validation rule it violated.

   •   Accepted record feedback: For successful submissions, the feedback will indicate the
       acceptance of the records that triggered no validation rules.

The feedback file is delivered to the SFTP location for each CASP and remains there for the same
period as the records and orders (7 years). Once the processing of a submission is finalized, the
feedback file is written to the [response] directory of the submitting CASP's SFTP account,
typically within 2 hours of receipt. CASPs retrieve it by connecting to their SFTP account and
downloading the file from this directory — the same connection used to submit files. No email is
sent, and no separate notification is generated; it is the CASPs' responsibility to check the
response directory. An automated polling job is recommended on a fixed interval rather than
manual checks. Details on the feedback file, along with a sample, are found in Annex 5.

3.5.4 Error correction process
   1. Review the feedback file promptly after every submission.
   2. Identify each rejected record and the rule it violated (record-level rejections) or the reason
       the file was rejected (file-level rejection).
   3. Correct the issues at source.
   4. Resubmit an updated version of the file that addresses all validation errors identified. For

9.1 Purpose and scope
9.1.1 What the feedback file is
The feedback file is the MIDAS system's response to a submission. It reports the outcome of
every validation rule defined in Annex 3 that was triggered by that submission, at both file level
and record level, together with the corrective action required of the submitting CASP.

It is the only channel through which MIDAS communicates the result of processing. A record
that is rejected is not held in MIDAS and will not appear in any surveillance output. Nothing
other than the feedback file will tell a CASP that this has happened.

9.1.2 1.2 What the feedback file is not
     •    It is not an acknowledgement of receipt. Successful transfer of a file to the SFTP
          endpoint is confirmed by the SFTP server at the point of transfer and is a separate matter
          from validation.
     •    It is not a notification. No email is sent and no alert is raised when a feedback file is
          written. It is the CASP's responsibility to retrieve it (see §2).
     •    It is not a correction mechanism. It reports what must be corrected; the correction itself
          is made at source and submitted in a subsequent file, in accordance with §3.5.4 of the
          Reporting Instructions.

9.1.3 1.3 Scope
The feedback file covers both report types:

     •    Executions (Record Keeping), auth.117 — mandatory for all CASPs.
     •    Order book, auth.118 — mandatory only for CASPs operating a trading platform for
          crypto-assets.

Not every validation rule applies to both report types. The AppliesTo column states the scope of
each rule, and §5.5 sets out which rules a given CASP can expect to receive.




                                                                                                 66
9.2 Delivery
9.2.1 When the feedback file is generated
MIDAS produces one feedback file per CASP per day. It is generated once MIDAS has
processed all files available from that CASP in that day's processing cycle, and reports on all of
them together.

Feedback is therefore not returned file by file as each submission is processed. A CASP that
submits several files receives a single consolidated response covering all of them.

9.2.2 Where the feedback file is written
The feedback file is deposited on the same SFTP endpoint from which the CASP's
submitted files were collected, in the same root folder in which they deposit their submitted
files. CASPs retrieve it using the same connection they use to submit data.

No email is sent and no separate notification is generated. It is the CASP’s responsibility to
check that folder after each submission. An automated polling job on a fixed interval is
recommended in preference to manual checks.

9.2.3 Relationship between submitted files and feedback files
The relationship is many to one. A single feedback file covers every file processed for that
CASP in that day's cycle, across both report types.

This differs from the one-submission-one-response pattern used by some other reporting
systems, and CASPs should account for it when building their handling:

   •   Submitting six part files for a single reporting date yields one feedback file, not six.
   •   Submitting an executions file and an order book file on the same day yields one feedback
       file covering both.
   •   Every row in the feedback file carries SubmittedFileName, which names the submitted file
       that row's finding was found in. This column is what attributes each finding back to its
       source file, and it is the only thing that does so.

Consequently a CASP must group findings by SubmittedFileName before acting on them.

9.2.4 Naming convention
Every feedback file is named [CASP NAME]_MIDAS_Feedback_File_yyyymmdd.csv, for
example KRAKEN_MIDAS_Feedback_File_20260908.csv.

[CASP NAME] is replaced with the submitting CASP's identifier. yyyymmdd is the date on which
MIDAS processed the batch, not the date the data relates to.



                                                                                                 67
Because MIDAS produces one feedback file per CASP per processing day, the name carries no
reference to any individual submitted file. A CASP that submits six part files on one day receives
one feedback file covering all six, and attribution of each finding back to its source file is carried
by the SubmittedFileName column rather than by the file name.

9.2.5 Retention
The feedback file remains in that same folder for the same period as the records and orders it
relates to, namely seven years.

9.2.6 Format
The feedback file is provided in CSV format, irrespective of the JSON format in which data is
submitted. Full specification at §3.1.

9.3.2 The governing principle: one row per finding, not one row per
      record
Everything else in this section follows from this.

A finding is a single validation rule firing against a single subject. The subject may be a record,
or it may be the file as a whole. Each finding produces exactly one row.

The consequences are:

   •   The file reports issues. It is not a manifest of the submission and does not carry a row for every
       record submitted.
   •   A record that breaches three rules produces three rows, all carrying the same RecordRef and the
       same JSONLineNumber, and differing in ErrorCode.
   •   Records that breach no rule are not listed individually. A single row reports the total number of
       records accepted into MIDAS, with Outcome = ACCEPTED_WITHOUT_WARNING.




                                                                                                        68
   •    A rule evaluated across the whole file produces one row with no RecordRef and no
        JSONLineNumber.
   •    A file rejected before processing produces one row per file-level rule breached, and no record-
        level rows at all, because no records were processed.

Therefore the number of rows in the feedback file bears no fixed relationship to the number of
records in the submission. Reconciliation is performed on RecordRef and JSONLineNumber, never
on row position.

Because warned records are listed individually and unwarned records are reported only as a
total, the submission reconciles exactly:

Total records submitted = the ACCEPTED_WITHOUT_WARNING count, plus the number of
distinct records carrying ACCEPTED_WITH_WARNING, plus the number of distinct records
carrying REJECTED.

The word distinct is load-bearing. A record breaching three rules produces three rows, so
counting rows rather than records would overstate the rejected population. Reconcile on
RecordRef together with JSONLineNumber, never on row count.

1. Search for `Outcome = FILE_REJECTED` first. If any row carries it, that file was rejected in its
   entirety and nothing in it was processed. No record-level rows will exist for that file. Correct and
   resubmit the file in full before considering anything else. You must notify the MIDAS support
   desk at support@esma.europa.eu when resubmitting, using the header format MIDAS - CASP -
   <CASP Name> - Topic. This is the same channel used for corrections to previously accepted
   records under section 3.5.4 of the Reporting Instructions.
2. Then `Outcome = REJECTED`. These records are not in MIDAS. Each must be corrected at
   source and resubmitted.
3. Then `Outcome = FILE_WARNING`. These indicate a systemic problem with the submission as
   a whole, typically upstream of the file itself. No resubmission is required, but the underlying
   cause should be investigated.
4. Then `Outcome = ACCEPTED_WITH_WARNING`. These records are in MIDAS. No
   resubmission is required. The value should be reviewed at source to prevent recurrence.
5. `Outcome = ACCEPTED_WITHOUT_WARNING` requires no action.

9.4.1 ReportTimestamp
The date and time at which the feedback file was generated, in YYYY-MM-DDThh:mm:ss.fff format,
UTC.

The value is identical on every row of a given feedback file. It is a property of the feedback file,
not of the individual finding, and it does not indicate when the underlying validation was
performed or when the submission was received.

                                                                                                        70
9.4.2 CaspIdentifier
The Legal Entity Identifier of the CASP whose submission is being reported on, as a 20-
character LEI conforming to ISO 17442.

Identical on every row of a given feedback file.

9.4.3 SubmittedFileName
The name of the submitted JSON file to which the row relates, exactly as it was received by
MIDAS, including the .json extension.

This column is the join key between a finding and the submission it came from, and it is the
mechanism by which a consolidated daily feedback file (§2.3) attributes each finding to the right
submission. Since a feedback file routinely reports on several submitted files, this is the only
column that distinguishes them. RecordRef and JSONLineNumber are scoped to the file named here
and are not unique across files.

Note that on FIL-101 rows the value reported is the offending file name itself, which by definition
does not conform to the required naming convention.

9.4.4 FeedbackType
The validation layer that produced the finding. See §5.1 for permitted values.

This is the first of four related but non-redundant status columns. Their division of labour is:

 Column                                             Answers
 FeedbackType                                       Which layer of validation ran?
 FileVerdict                                        What happened to the file?
 Outcome                                            What happened to this record?
 Severity                                           How serious is it?


Each is enumerated separately in §5, and §6 sets out which combinations can legitimately
occur.

9.4.5 FileVerdict
The disposition of the submitted file named in SubmittedFileName. See §5.2.

REJECTED means the file failed transmission or file validation and no records within it were
processed. PROCESSED means the file was accepted for processing; individual records within it
may still have been rejected.

FileVerdict is a property of the file, so it carries the same value on every row relating to the same
SubmittedFileName.

9.4.6 RecordRef
The business identifier of the record giving rise to the finding, taken from the record itself as
submitted.

The identifier used depends on the report type:

`RecordRef` is not necessarily unique within a file. This is a deliberate consequence of the

   •   In the order book, every event in an order's lifecycle carries the same Order identification code.

   •   In executions, a duplicate record by definition repeats the identifiers of the record it duplicates.
       Where MET-005 is reported, the same RecordRef will appear at more than one JSONLineNumber:
       the first instance, which is retained, and the subsequent instances, which are rejected.

Both cases are illustrated in §8.

9.4.7 JSONLineNumber
The line number within the submitted JSON file named in SubmittedFileName at which the record
giving rise to the finding is located. Counted from 1 at the first line of the file. Where a record
object spans several lines, the value is the opening line of that record object.

Empty on file-level rejection rows and on file-scope content warning rows.

Together with RecordRef, this column provides the audit trail from a finding back to the exact
record in the exact file the CASP sent, without the CASP needing to search its own data by
business key.

Because the value is a physical property of the file as transmitted, it depends on how the file is
laid out. A file submitted in pretty-printed form yields a precise and useful pointer. A file
submitted in minified form, with the entire JSON document on a single line, will produce 1 on



                                                                                                        72
every row and the column will carry no information. CASPs are therefore recommended to
submit pretty-printed JSON.

9.4.8 Outcome
The disposition of the subject of the finding — the record where one is identified, otherwise the
file. See §5.3.

This is the operative column for deciding what to do. §9 maps each value to the action required.

9.4.10          AppliesTo
The report type or types to which the validation rule identified in ErrorCode applies. See §5.5 for
permitted values and the full scope map.

Two properties of this column are easily misread and are stated here explicitly:

It describes the rule, not the row. AppliesTo = BOTH does not mean the row relates to both
reports; a row always relates to exactly one submitted file. It means the rule is capable of firing
against either report type. To determine which report a given row relates to, read
SubmittedFileName or LogicalPath.

It is a property of the error code and is constant. A given ErrorCode always carries the same
AppliesTo value, on every row, in every feedback file. The column is therefore redundant on any
individual row, and is included so that a CASP can determine, without consulting Annex 3,
which subset of rules its submissions are subject to. §5.5 gives that subset directly.

submitted Status was NEWO, which is what made it impermissible.

reports the Date and time against which the Validity period and time was found not to be later.

key on ErrorCode or Label.

9.7 Conventions
9.7.1 Empty, -, and literal values
Three states must be distinguished and must not be normalised to one another:

 State                                                 Assertion
 Empty cell                                            About the data. The field was absent or null in the
                                                       submission. On mandatory and conditional-
                                                       mandatory rules this is itself the finding.
 -                                                     About the row. The column is not applicable to this
                                                       kind of row.
 Any other string                                      The literal content of the field as submitted, including
                                                       strings such as N/A that may resemble placeholders.



9.7.2 Timestamps
All date and time values in the feedback file are given as YYYY-MM-DDThh:mm:ss.fff in UTC. Values
reproduced in SubmittedValue and ValidationReason are given as submitted and may not conform,
since non-conformance is frequently the finding being reported.

9.7.3 Composite rules
Where a validation rule operates on more than one field, Field, LogicalPath and SubmittedValue each
list the corresponding elements, in the same order. Read them positionally: the nth logical path
corresponds to the nth field name and the nth submitted value.


                                                                                                              80
LogicalPath joins its elements with + . Field and SubmittedValue use the separator shown in the row.

Where a rule operates on a composite key as a single concept rather than on its constituent
fields individually, Field gives a collective name for the key. MET-005 is the example: Field reads
Execution unique key, while LogicalPath and SubmittedValue enumerate the four elements that make up
the key.

9.7.4 Row order
Rows are presented with file-level findings first, then grouped by submitted file and ordered by
error code.

Row order is presentational and must not be relied upon by automated handling. Use ErrorCode,
SubmittedFileName, RecordRef and JSONLineNumber to locate and group findings.


9.7.5 Stability
The following are a stable contract and will not change without a versioned revision of these
instructions:

   •   The set and order of columns.
   •   The ErrorCode values and their meanings. Codes are not reused or renumbered.
   •   The Label values and their one-to-one mapping to error codes.
   •   The permitted values of FeedbackType, FileVerdict, Outcome, Severity and AppliesTo.

The following are not stable and must not be parsed:

   •   The wording of ValidationReason and CorrectiveAction.
   •   Row order.


9.8 Worked walkthrough of the sample
The accompanying sample, MIDAS_Feedback_File_Sample.csv, is a single feedback file covering
four submitted files from one CASP. It exercises every published validation rule. Each case
below refers to rows of that file.

9.8.1 A file rejected before processing
Two rows, both shape 1, reporting two separate submitted files.

CryptoAssetRecordKeeping_06082026_1_1.csv breaches FIL-101. The naming convention mandates the
.json extension and MIDAS accepts JSON submissions only, so a CSV file is rejected on its
name before its contents are examined. The required format is set out in §3.3; the same file
submitted as CryptoAssetRecordKeeping_06082026_1_1.json would have been accepted for
processing. Field reads SubmittedFileName and SubmittedValue reproduces the offending name.




                                                                                                  81
CryptoAssetRecordKeeping_06082026_2_2.json breaches FIL-102, a JSON schema conformance
failure. Because the failure is structural and pertains to the document as a whole, Field,
LogicalPath and SubmittedValue all read -.

In both cases FileVerdict is REJECTED and no record-level rows appear for those files anywhere in
the feedback file. Both require resubmission in full.

9.8.2 A processed file with rejected records
CryptoAssetRecordKeeping_06082026_1_1.json carries FileVerdict = PROCESSED throughout. Individual
records within it were nonetheless rejected.

Take the MET-001 row and follow it end to end:

 Column                               Value
 SubmittedFileName                    CryptoAssetRecordKeeping_06082026_1_1.json
 RecordRef                            1779321603054903072
 JSONLineNumber                       2
 Outcome                              REJECTED
 ErrorCode                            MET-001

 Field                                Crypto Asset Identification Code
 LogicalPath                          /Document/CrptAsstRcrdKeepgRpt/Tx/New/CrptAsst/DgtlTknIdr/DTI
 SubmittedValue                       (empty)


The CASP opens the named file, goes to line 2, confirms the record's Transaction Record
Number is 1779321603054903072, and finds the DTI element null. The empty SubmittedValue is not a
gap in the feedback, it is the finding. The record is not in MIDAS and must be corrected at
source and resubmitted on its own.

9.8.3 A record accepted but carrying a warning
The QUA-002 row reports a Trading platform for crypto-asset of PGSX, which does not resolve
against ISO 10383. Outcome is ACCEPTED_WITH_WARNING and Severity is WARNING.

The distinction from §8.2 is material: this record is in MIDAS and is available to surveillance. No
resubmission is required. The obligation is to correct the source so that later submissions carry
a valid MIC.

9.8.4 A whole-file quality warning with no record reference
Two rows, both shape 4, and the only rows in the file where a content validation carries no
record reference.

QUA-004 reports 620 BUYI / 17 SELL of 637 records in SubmittedValue, an aggregate, not a field value.
The execution buy/sell ratio of 97.3% to 2.7% suggests that only one leg of each trade is



                                                                                                    82
reaching MIDAS. RecordRef and JSONLineNumber are empty because no individual record is at
fault; the population is.

QUA-008 reports 128 of 637 records, 20.1% of Trading date time values falling exactly on midnight,
indicating timestamp defaulting upstream.

Neither requires resubmission. Both point at a systemic problem that will recur in every future
submission until it is fixed at source, which is why the corrective action directs investigation
rather than correction of the file.

9.8.5 An accepted record
One row, shape 5. FeedbackType is ACCEPTED_RECORD, Outcome is
ACCEPTED_WITHOUT_WARNING and Severity is INFO. RecordRef and JSONLineNumber
are empty because the row is an aggregate rather than a reference to one record, ErrorCode,
Field and LogicalPath read - because no rule fired and no field is implicated, and
SubmittedValue carries the count of records accepted.

This row exists because §3.5.3 requires the feedback file to confirm the acceptance of records
that triggered no validation rule.

The feedback file is issue-driven: it is not a per-record manifest of the submission. It does not
carry one row per accepted record, and a CASP submitting 100,000 records of which three are
defective should not expect 100,000 rows. Acceptance is confirmed on the basis set out above;
absence of a finding against a record is itself confirmation that the record was accepted.

9.8.7 Locating a record where RecordRef repeats
Two cases in the sample show why RecordRef alone is not sufficient.

Order lifecycle. Ord_1779321887089541910 appears at lines 1, 2 and 32 of the order book file.
These are three separate records, successive events in the life of one order, all carrying the
same Order identification code. Findings at line 2 (MET-009, MET-012, MET-014, MET-015)
relate to a different record from those at line 1 in §8.6.

Duplicates. In the executions file, RecordRef 1779321603054903058 appears at line 4 carrying MET-
005, and also at line 1. Line 4 is the duplicate and was rejected; line 1 is where the first instance
sits. This is the expected shape of a duplicate finding, and it is the reason MET-005 does not
call for resubmission, the record's content is already in MIDAS, submitted at line 1.

9.8.8 Coverage of the sample
The sample exercises all 26 published validation rules, FIL-101 and FIL-102, MET-001 through
MET-016, and QUA-001 through QUA-008, together with one accepted-record row. It is
intended to give CASPs a concrete reference against which to build and test their handling of
the feedback file. It is illustrative: the values in it are constructed, and it does not represent the
volume or distribution of findings that a real submission would produce.

9.9.1 Deriving the resubmission set

                                     recurrence.

Where a file yields FILE_REJECTED, do not look for record-level rows: there are none, because no

9.9.2 Automated handling
CASPs intending to process the feedback file programmatically should observe the following:

   •   Key on `ErrorCode` or `Label`. Both are stable. Never key on ValidationReason or CorrectiveAction.
   •   Determine the action from `Outcome`, qualified by ErrorCode for the MET-005 case above.
   •   Group findings by `SubmittedFileName` + `RecordRef` + `JSONLineNumber` to obtain the set
       of findings per record. Grouping by RecordRef alone is incorrect for order book submissions,
       where lifecycle events share an order identification code.
   •   Treat an empty SubmittedValue and a - as different values (see §7.1).
   •   Do not rely on row order (see §7.4).
   •   Assert incoming rows against the five legal shapes in §6; any other combination indicates a
       parsing error.
